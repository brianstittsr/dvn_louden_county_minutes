  Loudoun Board Advances Review of Briskman-TeKrony
      Effort to End 2025 Grandfathering Exceptions

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

LEESBURG, VA, September 2 – The Loudoun County Board of Supervisors voted
unanimously Tuesday to direct County staff to return to the October 6 Board Business
Meeting with legal analysis of a potential amendment to end the County’s 2025 Data Center
Grandfathering Resolution.

The action advances the initiative introduced by Supervisors Juli E. Briskman and Laura A.
TeKrony seeking to end the 2025 data center grandfathering exceptions that allow certain
pending administrative data center applications to continue through the prior by-right
approval process without obtaining the Special Exception now generally required for
proposed data centers.
However, the final motion adopted by the Board narrowed the original proposal by removing
the direction for County staff to prepare the amendment for consideration on October 6. As
a result, even if the legal analysis supports moving forward, final Board action could be
delayed beyond that date.
“Thanks to the initiative Supervisor TeKrony and I brought forward, every member of the
Board voted to seek legal analysis on ending these grandfathering exceptions,” said
Supervisor Briskman. “I am disappointed, however, that the final motion stopped short of
having an amendment to the grandfathering resolution ready for consideration on October
6 alongside the legal analysis. If that analysis confirms the Board has the authority to act,
we should be prepared to act swiftly.”

“Data center applications should never be approved by-right without a public hearing and
Board review. I did not support the grandfathering resolution in March 2025 and believe we
need to do the right thing for the community and expire this resolution now. We’ve given the
data center industry 25 years to build by-right. There is no legal requirement for
grandfathering. It is a choice,” said Supervisor TeKrony. “I am disappointed that we will not
have an amended resolution to approve on October 6. When the item comes back to the
Board, I will be pushing for an expiration date as soon as possible.”
Seventeen administrative data center applications are currently in review and have been
identified as grandfathered under the 2025 resolution. They represent approximately 11
million square feet of development. Each application usually has multiple data center
buildings in its site plan.
The 2025 Data Center Grandfathering Resolution was adopted on March 18, 2025 by a 5-4
vote (Briskman, Glass, TeKrony, and Turner opposed) alongside Phase 1 of the Data Center
Standards and Locations initiative, which generally ended by-right data center development
for new applications. However, the grandfathering resolution allows certain data center site
plans and amendments to continue through the by-right administrative review process
without obtaining the Special Exception now required for other proposed data centers. This
means they do not need to participate in public hearings or seek a final vote by the Board.

At the March 18, 2025 meeting, Supervisor Briskman introduced a motion to remove
administrative applications from the resolution, requiring those projects to undergo public
hearings and a final public vote by the Board of Supervisors. That motion ultimately failed 4-
5 (Kershner, Letourneau, Randall, Saines, and Umstattd opposed).

Until the Board takes further action, the 2025 Grandfathering Resolution remains in effect.
The Board will next consider Supervisor Briskman’s separate proposal concerning a
potential pause on data center and substation applications at its September 15 Business
Meeting.

Supervisor Juli E. Briskman was first elected in 2019 to represent the Algonkian District on
the Board of Supervisors and was reelected in 2023. She served as Vice Chair of the Board
in 2024 and as Chair of the Finance, Government Operations, and Economic Development
Committee for nearly two years. Briskman is a proud mom of two Loudoun County Public
Schools alumni and is a graduate of The Ohio State University. She also holds an MBA from
Johns Hopkins University. Briskman represented Loudoun on the Metropolitan Washington
Council of Governments for four years and currently serves on the Northern Virginia
Transportation Commission and the Virginia Association of Counties Board of Directors.
Supervisor Laura A. TeKrony was elected to represent the Little River District on the Board of
Supervisors in November 2023. She serves as Chair of the Board of Supervisors'
Transportation and Land Use Committee and is a member of the Joint Board and School
Board Committee. She is one of Loudoun's representatives on the Metropolitan Washington
Council of Governments' (COG) Board of Directors. On COG she serves on the Climate,
Energy and Environment Policy Committee (CEEPC) and is Vice Chair of the Food and
Agriculture Regional Member Policy Committee. Supervisor TeKrony also serves on Virginia
Association of Counties Agriculture and Rural Affairs Steering Committee.
