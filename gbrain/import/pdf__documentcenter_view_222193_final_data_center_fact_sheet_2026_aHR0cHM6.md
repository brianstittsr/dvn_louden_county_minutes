 DATA CENTER FACT SHEET
Loudoun County is home to one of the largest concentrations of data centers in the world through
which much of the world’s internet traffic passes every day. The rapid development of the data center
industry in Loudoun County has resulted in economic benefits and concerns among residents about
the impact of data centers on the surrounding community. Over the past three decades, the county and
community have learned valuable lessons about data center development as Loudoun County’s data
center story continues to evolve.

                  DATA CENTERS IN LOUDOUN COUNTY TIMELINE
  1990s       America Online and UUNet each locate their headquarters in Ashburn as the Metropolitan Area
              Exchange East (MAE East), brings miles of fiber infrastructure to Loudoun County.
  2000        Loudoun County’s zoning administrator classifies data centers as similar to offices from a zoning
              perspective and determines they can be built by-right in any area where office buildings
              are allowed.

  2008        In response to the crash of the housing market and the resulting loss of real estate tax revenue,
              Loudoun County works to diversify its tax base by focusing its economic development strategy
              on technology infrastructure.

  2016        Loudoun County becomes home to the largest concentration of data centers in the world.

  2023        Through the process to adopt a new zoning ordinance, Loudoun County strengthens design
              requirements applying to new data center development, and identifies data centers as an
              item for additional consideration through further amendments to the Zoning Ordinance and
              Comprehensive Plan.
  2024        The Board of Supervisors initiates a two-phase project to update policies and standards for
              data center and electrical substation uses.
  2025        In Phase 1 of the project, the Board of Supervisors ends by-right development of most data
              centers, meaning that future proposed data centers must go through a legislative review process
              including public input. A grandfathering resolution allows certain pending applications to
              continue through the by-right process.
  2026        Phase 2 of the project is underway, focusing on establishing new potential policy guidance and
              new use-specific zoning standards for data centers and utility substations. The FY 2027 adopted
              budget projects data center revenue at $1.3 billion, representing 40% of the total budget.

           TOP CONSIDERATIONS FOR DATA CENTER DEVELOPMENT
At the outset, it was difficult for Loudoun County to foresee what the data center industry would
become, how infrastructure would evolve and how it would impact residents over time. As data center
density has increased, residents raised concerns about a wide variety of impacts. Here are the top 10
lessons learned:

       Siting: Carefully consider where you want to permit data center development, and whether data
  1    center development should be permitted by right or as a conditional approval.
       Energy: Planning appropriately for the energy needs of data centers is as important as planning
  2    for the siting, design, and operation of the data centers themselves.
       Electrical Infrastructure: If there is not adequate electrical infrastructure to support data centers,
  3    new high-voltage transmission lines and towers, along with distribution substations, may need to
       be constructed.
       Water: Local water systems are cyclical and managed with precision. Any new data center
  4    connecting to municipal water supplies or wastewater systems should be evaluated for its
       potential impact on the overall water cycle, in both the near- and long-term.
       Noise: Data centers can produce noticeable noise from a variety of sources. When considering
  5    proposed data center projects, take adjacent land uses into account and work with the data
       center companies and the community to mitigate noise concerns as early as possible.
       Land Values: Data centers are big businesses, and their presence can have an impact on the
  6    value of land. Be mindful of the potential for data centers to make land unaffordable to other
       commercial or residential developers.
       Design Standards: Data center buildings are large, industrial facilities that can dominate
       viewsheds. Communities should plan either for appropriate buffering, screening, and setbacks,
  7    or should work with data center developers to ensure the buildings match the aesthetic of the
       surrounding buildings.
       Secure Facilities: Data centers are secure facilities, therefore county officials need to make
  8    arrangements with data center staff in advance for routine access for inspections and for public
       safety response.
       Fire Response: Data centers often use fire suppression systems that are not reliant on water-
  9    based solutions, due to the sensitivity of the equipment within. Local first responders will need
       specialized education, training, and equipment to respond to an emergency in this environment.
       Tax Revenue: Use data center revenue strategically to support capital improvement projects and
 10    one-time expenditures, rather than ongoing or recurring expenses, to avoid becoming reliant on
       a revenue stream that may change over time.
