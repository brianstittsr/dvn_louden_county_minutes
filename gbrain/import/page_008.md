Under the 1972 and 1993 Zoning Ordinances, data center uses were not specifically defined, but
through interpretation they were permitted by right as an office use. The Revised 1993 Zoning
Ordinance established use specific standards for data centers. As part of the ZOR process, staff
prepared strengthened use-specific standards regulating data centers to minimize the potential
impact on adjacent properties and roadways. Additional requirements govern building façade
treatments and landscaping, and new standards require data center applicants to submit lighting
plans and pre-and post-construction noise modeling to ensure conformance to lighting and noise
standards. The adopted data center use-specific standards are attached for reference (Attachment
4).

During the ZOR review, the Planning Commission (Commission) and Board focused on the
provision of standards to minimize impacts of data centers on adjacent residential uses.
Specifically, the Zoning Ordinance requires data centers be set back 200 linear feet from residential
uses. The Zoning Ordinance limits generator testing timeframes and limits the use of generators to
back up/emergency uses, exclusive of any use establishment activities. The Zoning Ordinance also
includes additional buffer standards for data centers, which require the installation of a Buffer
Type C with specific plant unit composition requirements, an increase in buffer width and number
of plant units per 100 linear feet, and the provision of an earthen berm that has a minimum height
of six feet. The new zoning standards provide additional protections for neighboring properties
from impacts associated with building scale, aesthetics, and noise that did not exist in the Revised
1993 Zoning Ordinance.

This draft ZOAM will consider the adopted use specific standards and determine if revised or
additional standards are necessary. These additional standards may include, but are not limited to,
noise attenuation measures, building and site design, landscaping and buffering, accessory use
requirements, and for establishment of the data center uses within existing structures.

The draft CPAM and ZOAM will be processed together to coordinate research efforts,
development of policy guidance and new Zoning Ordinance standards, and stakeholder
engagement. This wholistic approach provides greater efficiency and ensures that the CPAM
provides adequate policy support to enable implementation of the ZOAM.

The purpose of this item is to provide a Project Plan for the Data Center Standards and Locations

BACKGROUND
The Data Center Standards and Locations CPAM and ZOAM build upon previous Board of
Supervisors (Board) discussions and decisions that began in the Spring of 2022. The Board’s
Transportation and Land Use Committee (TLUC) held a three-part Data Center Discussion Series on
April 20, June 1, and July 18, 2022, on targeted revisions to policies and regulations concerning data
center uses throughout Loudoun County. The results and recommendations from the three-part TLUC
Data Center Discussion Series were presented at the September 20, 2022, Board Business Meeting. The
Board affirmed that data centers should not be permitted in the Urban Transit Center, Urban Mixed Use,
Urban Employment, Suburban Mixed Use, Suburban Compact Neighborhood, and Suburban
Neighborhood Place Types. Further, the Board concluded that data centers should be allowed by legislative
approval within the Suburban Employment Place Type and as by-right use within the Suburban Industrial/

the following Project Plan for the Data Center Standards and Locations CPAM and ZOAM.

stakeholder outreach and engagement. The interrelationship between policy guidance in the 2019 GP
