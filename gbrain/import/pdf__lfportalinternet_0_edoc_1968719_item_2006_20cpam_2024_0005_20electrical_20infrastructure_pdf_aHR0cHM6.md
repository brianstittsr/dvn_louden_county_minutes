PURPOSE: The purpose of this item is to consider a Comprehensive Plan Amendment (CPAM)
proposing amendments to the Loudoun County 2019 General Plan (2019 GP) to provide map, text,
and policy amendments related to the preferred location, siting, design, and aesthetics of high
voltage transmission corridors (defined as any electric line with a voltage equal to or greater than
138 kilovolts (kV)) and electrical infrastructure. The CPAM includes revisions to Chapter 6,
Chapter 7, the Glossary, and such other policies, maps, and provisions of the 2019 GP as necessary
to implement and maintain consistency with the proposed amendments or as otherwise necessary
to correct typographical errors, section and subsection numbering, and formatting; update cross-
references; and further clarify the requirements of the 2019 GP (Attachment 1).

Draft Amendments to Electrical Polices of the 2019 GP: The energy policies in the 2019 GP
provide general guidance on the preferred location and design of high voltage transmission
corridors and electrical infrastructure. The proposed draft electrical infrastructure policy
amendments in Chapter 6, Chapter 7, and the Glossary build on and expand the guiding principles
and existing energy policies in the 2019 GP. The draft policies acknowledge that high voltage
transmission lines and electrical infrastructure are necessary to ensure the structural integrity and
reliability of the County’s electrical network to serve existing business and residential uses and
support economic development.

The draft policies reiterate the County’s longstanding position of identifying and supporting the
colocation of new high voltage transmission lines within existing high voltage corridors as the
County’s first preference to expand capacity before considering alternative routing of new
transmission corridors. Colocating new transmission lines and electrical infrastructure within
existing overhead high voltage transmission corridors minimizes environmental impacts and the
overall visual impact on the surrounding community by grouping these tall structures within an
existing and/or expanded transmission corridor right-of-way (ROW).

The draft policies propose the inclusion and adoption of an Electrical Infrastructure Map (see
Figure 1) identifying all existing and approved high voltage transmission corridors designated by
the County as a component of the 2019 GP. The inclusion of the Electrical Infrastructure Map will
identify and direct electrical providers and governmental decision-makers to locations where the
County would anticipate and support the colocation, expansion, and development of new high

Chapter 6 - Fiscal Management & Public Infrastructure
Energy
Electrical demand in the region has steadily increased with the growth of business and
residential development and the continued expansion of the data center industry. Data centers
are among the highest consumers of electricity within the County. Electric providers in recent
years have completed upgrades to existing high voltage transmission corridors and have
received and/or are seeking approvals for other routes in the County to deliver bulk electricity
to the region and to concentrations of data centers where existing and future load growth are
forecasted. The County and electrical providers anticipate that additional construction and/or
upgrades to the existing high voltage transmission network will be required in portions of the
County to meet current and forecasted electric demand associated with future development.
These improvements to the transmission network are required to ensure that federally
mandated reliability standards for the electric grid are maintained within certain limits if one
or more components of the transmission network were to fail.

High voltage electrical transmission lines are crucial to the delivery of large volumes of bulk
electricity over long distances from generating facilities to local utility substations where the
high voltage electricity is reduced to a lower voltage before being conveyed through a network
of distribution lines to the end-users within the community. In Virginia, the Commonwealth
regulates the placement of high voltage transmission lines, which are electrical lines with
voltages equal to or greater than 138 kilovolts (kV). High voltage transmission lines are
generally constructed overhead on galvanized metal lattice, H-frame, or monopole towers that
range in height from 125 feet to 180 feet and require a cleared right-of-way (ROW) that is
typically the approximate height of the towers. High voltage transmission lines may be placed
underground in some instances, though construction costs are higher, underground utilities,
fiber, roadways, and environmental conditions may pose engineering challenges, and the
maintenance, operation, and repair of the buried lines are more complex. As such, underground
lines represent only a small percentage of the number of high voltage transmission lines within
Virginia.

As of 2025, approximately 37 miles of 500 kV transmission lines, 125 miles of 230 kV
transmission lines, and ten miles of 138 kV transmission lines have been constructed overhead,
and two miles of 230 kV transmission lines have been constructed underground in the County.
The backbone of the County’s transmission system is the 500 kV and 230 kV lines that run
north to south through the middle of the County. The primary 230 kV transmission lines
serving portions of eastern and western Loudoun are located proximate to portions of the
Washington and Old Dominion Trail, the Broad Run, VA Route 7, US Route 50, and through
“Data Center Alley” adjoining Waxpool Road and Loudoun County Parkway (see Electrical
Infrastructure Map). Rather than a centralized, regional substation to serve the County’s
growing electrical demands, smaller substations have been constructed to serve geographic
areas and individual high energy users. As demand for increased electricity continues, greater
consideration should be given to the location and appearance of transmission lines, substations,
and distribution lines to provide adequate siting and screening of these facilities to reduce the

Electrical services are provided under the purview of state and federal agencies, which limits
the County’s ability to mitigate impacts of electrical infrastructure. For example, the County
regulates the location of electrical substations but not the high voltage transmission lines to
and from the substations. In Virginia, the State Corporation Commission (SCC) regulates the
location and construction of high voltage transmission lines through the issuance of a
Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity.1 When reviewing and approving applications
for high voltage transmission corridors and facilities, the SCC considers several criteria, which
include the need for the transmission line and the impact on the reliability of electric service;
impacts on the environment, including scenic assets and historic and cultural resources;
impacts on economic development; and, if requested by the County in which facilities are
proposed to be built, duly adopted local comprehensive plans.2 Electric transmission lines of
138 kV or more, when approved by the SCC are deemed to satisfy the requirements of the
Zoning Ordinance.3

The Loudoun County 2019 General Plan includes an Electrical Infrastructure Map that
identifies all existing and approved high voltage transmission corridors and supporting policies
that serve as a guide for the placement and development of electrical infrastructure. To
minimize community and environmental impacts, the policies establish the County’s
preference to colocate and/or expand existing and approved transmission corridors where
feasible. The County also supports the deployment of new technologies, such as smart grids
and advanced conductors, to optimize the ability of the existing high voltage electric
transmission network to efficiently serve the County and limit the need for new transmission
corridors. The County’s electrical infrastructure is anticipated to evolve through the integration
of renewable electric sources, energy storage facilities, and electric generation facilities that
enhance the supply of electricity, as well as other technologies that improve the performance,
efficiency, and reliability of the electrical network.

The expansion and development of the County’s electrical infrastructure to address future load
growth and ensure the reliability of the network requires deliberate planning and collaboration
between both the County and electrical providers. The electrical policies provide guidance on
the location, siting, design, and aesthetics of high voltage transmission corridors and electrical
infrastructure that align with County and community objectives to minimize adverse effects on
environmental, historic, and natural resources as well as existing and proposed land uses. The
electrical policies serve as a guide for electrical providers, County staff, elected officials, and
other governmental decision-makers when planning and considering where and how high
voltage transmission corridors and improvements to the electrical network are constructed and
managed within the County.

Communication
Broadband internet service is an increasingly important asset to business in Loudoun as e-
commerce grows throughout the nation. The lack of broadband service in western Loudoun is
cited as a major constraint on the rural economy. It also puts western households and students
in particular at a disadvantage. County efforts to extend broadband service have included
regulatory changes to support new technologies. With limited control over market factors and
federal regulation, the County will encourage landowners to install conduits and other
infrastructure to help minimize the cost of extending the service and explore other incentives to
encourage network expansion. The County may review the location of cell towers and
monopoles for impacts on surrounding properties, but cannot prescribe locations and,
therefore, cannot require broadband or communication service in underserved areas. The
County does, however, work with providers to encourage improved service and locations.

Chapter 6 - Fiscal Management & Public Infrastructure
Energy
Electrical demand in the region has steadily increased with the growth of business and
residential development and the continued expansion of the data center industry. Data centers
are among the highest consumers of electricity within the County. Electric providers in recent
years have completed upgrades to existing high voltage transmission corridors and have
received and/or are seeking approvals for other routes in the County to deliver bulk electricity
to the region and to concentrations of data centers where existing and future load growth are
forecasted. The County and electrical providers anticipate that additional construction and/or
upgrades to the existing high voltage transmission network will be required in portions of the
County to meet current and forecasted electric demand associated with future development.
These improvements to the transmission network are required to ensure that federally
mandated reliability standards for the electric grid are maintained within certain limits if one
or more components of the transmission network were to fail.

High voltage electrical transmission lines are crucial to the delivery of large volumes of bulk
electricity over long distances from generating facilities to local utility substations where the
high voltage electricity is reduced to a lower voltage before being conveyed through a network
of distribution lines to the end-users within the community. In Virginia, the Commonwealth
regulates the placement of high voltage transmission lines, which are electrical lines with
voltages equal to or greater than 138 kilovolts (kV). High voltage transmission lines are
generally constructed overhead on galvanized metal lattice, H-frame, or monopole towers that
range in height from 125 feet to 180 feet and require a cleared right-of-way (ROW) that is
typically the approximate height of the towers. High voltage transmission lines may be placed
underground in some instances, though construction costs are higher, underground utilities,
fiber, roadways, and environmental conditions may pose engineering challenges, and the
maintenance, operation, and repair of the buried lines are more complex. As such, underground
lines represent only a small percentage of the number of high voltage transmission lines within
Virginia.

As of 2025, approximately 37 miles of 500 kV transmission lines, 125 miles of 230 kV
transmission lines, and ten miles of 138 kV transmission lines have been constructed overhead,
and two miles of 230 kV transmission lines have been constructed underground in the County.
The backbone of the County’s transmission system is the 500 kV and 230 kV lines that run
north to south through the middle of the County. The primary 230 kV transmission lines
serving portions of eastern and western Loudoun are located proximate to portions of the
Washington and Old Dominion Trail, the Broad Run, VA Route 7, US Route 50, and through
“Data Center Alley” adjoining Waxpool Road and Loudoun County Parkway (see Electrical
Infrastructure Map). Rather than a centralized, regional substation to serve the County’s

Electrical services are provided under the purview of state and federal agencies, which limits
the County’s ability to mitigate impacts of electrical infrastructure. For example, the County
regulates the location of electrical substations but not the high voltage transmission lines to
and from the substations. In Virginia, the State Corporation Commission (SCC) regulates the
location and construction of high voltage transmission lines through the issuance of a
Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity.1 When reviewing and approving applications
for high voltage transmission corridors and facilities, the SCC considers several criteria, which
include the need for the transmission line and the impact on the reliability of electric service;
impacts on the environment, including scenic assets and historic and cultural resources;
impacts on economic development; and, if requested by the County in which facilities are
proposed to be built, duly adopted local comprehensive plans.2 Electric transmission lines of
138 kV or more, when approved by the SCC are deemed to satisfy the requirements of the
Zoning Ordinance.3

The Loudoun County 2019 General Plan includes an Electrical Infrastructure Map that
identifies all existing and approved high voltage transmission corridors and supporting policies
that serve as a guide for the placement and development of electrical infrastructure. To
minimize community and environmental impacts, the policies establish the County’s
preference to colocate and/or expand existing and approved transmission corridors where
feasible. The County also supports the deployment of new technologies, such as smart grids
and advanced conductors, to optimize the ability of the existing high voltage electric
transmission network to efficiently serve the County and limit the need for new transmission
corridors. The County’s electrical infrastructure is anticipated to evolve through the integration
of renewable electric sources, energy storage facilities, and electric generation facilities that
enhance the supply of electricity, as well as other technologies that improve the performance,
efficiency, and reliability of the electrical network.

The expansion and development of the County’s electrical infrastructure to address future load
growth and ensure the reliability of the network requires deliberate planning and collaboration
between both the County and electrical providers. The electrical policies provide guidance on
the location, siting, design, and aesthetics of high voltage transmission corridors and electrical
infrastructure that align with County and community objectives to minimize adverse effects on
environmental, historic, and natural resources as well as existing and proposed land uses. The
electrical policies serve as a guide for electrical providers, County staff, elected officials, and
other governmental decision-makers when planning and considering where and how high
voltage transmission corridors and improvements to the electrical network are constructed and
managed within the County.

Communication
Broadband internet service is an increasingly important asset to business in Loudoun as e-
commerce grows throughout the nation. The lack of broadband service in western Loudoun is
cited as a major constraint on the rural economy. It also puts western households and students
in particular at a disadvantage. County efforts to extend broadband service have included
regulatory changes to support new technologies. With limited control over market factors and
federal regulation, the County will encourage landowners to install conduits and other
infrastructure to help minimize the cost of extending the service and explore other incentives to
encourage network expansion. The County may review the location of cell towers and
monopoles for impacts on surrounding properties, but cannot prescribe locations and,
therefore, cannot require broadband or communication service in underserved areas. The
County does, however, work with providers to encourage improved service and locations.

 Integrated Vegetation Management (IVM): The practice of promoting desirable, stable,
 low-growing plant communities that will resist invasion by tall growing tree species using
 appropriate, environmentally sound, and cost-effective control methods.
