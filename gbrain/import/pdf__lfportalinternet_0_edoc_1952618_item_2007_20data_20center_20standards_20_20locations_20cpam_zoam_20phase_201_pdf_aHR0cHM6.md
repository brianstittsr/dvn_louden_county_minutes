development, impacts to industries related to data center development, and the potential for more

proposed amendments, citing the importance of regulating data center development to mitigate
cumulative potential adverse impacts on the environment, residential neighborhoods, and

new data centers; the need for greater economic diversification in the county; and the loss of land
available for other uses including flex and warehouse.

Motion 1: Staff Analysis
At the Board Business Meeting on March 4, 2025, staff informed the Board that 21 administrative
applications would qualify to be grandfathered if the previous draft Grandfathering Resolution was
approved. 1 Additionally, nine administrative applications received legislative approval for a data
center use from the Board between 2015 and 2022 that would have qualified under the draft
Grandfathering Resolution provided on March 4, for a total of 31 administrative applications that
would have qualified for grandfathering. 2,3 The contemplated provision requiring a 500-foot
distance between data centers and residential uses in order for an application to qualify for
grandfathering would provide the Board an opportunity to review and, if necessary, mitigate the
impacts of conflicting land uses caused by the size, height, area, bulk, and location of data center
structures, the noise generated from such structures, and the obstruction of light, air, and view by
such structures. This proposed provision is also consistent with Virginia Senate Bill 1449, recently
approved by the Virginia General Assembly but not yet signed by the Governor. If enacted, this
bill would require applicants for high energy use facilities such as data centers to perform and
submit a site assessment to examine the sound profile of the use on residential units and schools
located within 500 feet of the data center property boundary prior to any approval of a rezoning
application or SPEX application.

Staff conducted an analysis to determine the number of applications within 500 feet of residential
uses to better determine the impacts of Grandfathering Motion 1. A 500-foot buffer was created
around the 31 properties subject to active administrative applications. The 500-foot buffer was
measured from the proposed structures and external mechanical equipment for the data center use
to the nearest existing residential structure. The findings of that analysis are provided below.

separation between data centers and residential uses as this provision not yet been added to the draft Grandfathering
Resolution.

It is important to note that the above analysis was conducted within a limited timeframe and may
be constrained by the availability of information. Staff have made their best effort to accurately
categorize the affected data center applications as outlined above. However, if the Grandfathering
Resolution and ZOAM are adopted, there will be three different legal mechanisms that may allow
an ongoing data center application to be approved without requesting a SPEX. Those mechanisms
are the following: the Grandfathering Resolution (Attachment 4), footnote 4 to Table 3.02.05-1
(Attachment 3), and vested rights. An applicant could use the provisions of Zoning Ordinance
Section 10.02 to seek a determination from the County on whether any of these mechanisms are
applicable to a specific application. Additionally, the Grandfathering Resolution requires evidence
to be submitted for evaluation by the Office of Mapping and Geographic Information to determine
if a data center structure is more than 500 feet from a residential structure, which provides an
opportunity for applicants to advocate that their application meets the requirement.

Staff also notes that the ZOAM under consideration proposes changes only to the current Zoning
Ordinance. With the adoption of the current Zoning Ordinance at the December 13, 2023, Board
Public Hearing, the Board approved a Grandfathering Resolution for that effort that established
grandfathering provisions to allow qualifying pending development applications to be approved
under the Revised 1993 Loudoun County Zoning Ordinance (Revised 1993 Zoning Ordinance).
Therefore, any active applications grandfathered to the 1993 Revised Zoning Ordinance would not
be subject to this amendment provided they maintain their grandfathering status.

The draft amendment to Zoning Ordinance Table 3.02.05-1 (Attachment 3) has been updated to
include an additional footnote stating: “ Notwithstanding Sections 10.10.01.G and 10.10.05.G, any

Data Center Development in Loudoun County: As of February 12, 2025, Planning and Zoning
staff calculated that Loudoun County has approximately 46 million square feet (SF) of data centers
constructed or with a building permit issued and about 61.5 million SF of potential data center
development. The potential development includes all possible data center square footage
associated with the legislative and administrative applications submitted to the County. However,
it is important to note that this is an unconstrained number and that not all possible data center
square footage may be fully realized due to various factors including but not limited to changes in
political landscape affecting the industry, changes in technology, limitations in electric
transmission capacity and related infrastructure, and market competitions internal and external to
the county. The Department of Economic Development’s pipeline calculations considering these
constraints show that Loudoun County has approximately 43 million SF of existing data center
constructed, with another 6 million SF under development, 5 and approximates the data center
pipeline at an additional 21–26 million SF for a maximum of 70–75 million SF, which is consistent
with the adopted Fiscal Impact Committee report in 2024.

    RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF SUPERVISORS OF LOUDOUN COUNTY,
  VIRGINIA, ADOPTING GRANDFATHERING RULES FOR ZOAM-2024-0001, DATA
               CENTER STANDARDS AND LOCATIONS, PHASE I

         WHEREAS, on July 2, 2024, the Board of Supervisors of Loudoun County, Virginia
 (“Board”), adopted a resolution initiating amendments to the existing Loudoun County Zoning
 Ordinance (“Zoning Ordinance”) for the purpose of requiring approval of a special exception prior
 to the establishment of a data center use in the Industrial Park (IP), General Industry (GI), and
 Mineral Resource – Heavy Industry (MR-HI) zoning districts; and

       WHEREAS, the project to amend the zoning ordinance is known as ZOAM-2024-0001,
 Data Center Standards and Locations, Phase 1; and

         WHEREAS, after a period of extensive public engagement, including input from affected
 regulatory agencies and departments, stakeholder groups, residents, business owners, and other
 interested parties, County staff prepared draft zoning ordinance text amendments (“Text
 Amendments”) for consideration by the Planning Commission; and

        WHEREAS, the Planning Commission conducted one public hearing on September 24,
 2024, and 6 work sessions on the Text Amendments over a period of approximately four months;
 and

         WHEREAS, on December 12, 2024, the Planning Commission forwarded (5-3-1: Barnes,
 Frank, and Keirce opposed; Jasper absent) the Text Amendments to the Board with a
 recommendation of denial, and with the further recommendation that the Board adopt a
 grandfathering resolution effective as of the Board public hearing date if the Text Amendments are
 to be adopted; and

       WHEREAS, on February 12, 2025, the Board conducted a public hearing on the Text
 Amendments and further discussed the Text Amendments and grandfathering resolution at its
 March 4, 2025, and March 18, 2025, Business Meetings; and

       WHEREAS, on March 18, 2025, the Board adopted the final Text Amendments, as amended
by any changes or corrections approved by the Board at the time of adoption; and

        WHEREAS, the Board has determined that the orderly transition from the prior provisions of
Table 3.02.05-1 of the Zoning Ordinance to the new provisions for Table 3.02.05-1 of the Zoning
Ordinance (“New Table”) would be assisted by the implementation of rules allowing development
applications for a data center use that were officially accepted for review, in accordance with
applicable County ordinances and policies, on or before February 12, 2025, to be processed without
requiring a special exception for the data center use (“Grandfathering Rules”); and

         WHEREAS, Section 1.02.G. of the Zoning Ordinance provides, in part, that the “Board of
 Supervisors may establish grandfathering provisions by resolution adopted contemporaneously
 with this Zoning Ordinance or any amendment”; and

        WHEREAS, the County officially accepting a legislative or administrative application for
 review is the earliest point in the review and approval process that balances the reasonable
 expectations of property owners seeking development approvals with the need to adopt zoning
                                                                                   Page 1 of 5
                                         Attachment 4

ordinance amendments to protect other property owners and the community from the adverse
impacts of conflicting land uses; and

        WHEREAS, requiring a minimum 500-foot buffer from a proposed data center structure to
a residential structure existing on the Effective Date (defined below) is good land use and zoning
practice that provides residential areas with healthy surroundings for family life, promotes an
attractive, convenient and harmonious community, protects the health, safety and general welfare
of the public, and provides a minimum buffer area to mitigate the impacts of conflicting land uses
caused by the size, height, area, bulk, and location of data center structures, the noise generated
from such structures, and the obstruction of light, air, and view by such structures.

       NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of Supervisors of Loudoun County,
Virginia that:

       1.     Subject to the terms and conditions of this resolution, applicants of a legislative
application for a data center use that has been officially accepted for review in accordance with
applicable Loudoun County ordinances and policies on or before February 12, 2025 (“Effective
Date”), may continue to process their legislative application without applying for a special
exception for the data center use, provided the applicant does not make any substantial
modification(s) to such legislative application (as defined in Paragraph 7 below). Upon approval,
a grandfathered legislative application will be treated for purposes of the Zoning Ordinance as if
the legislative application had been approved prior to the Effective Date.

       2.      Subject to the terms and conditions of this resolution, applicants of an administrative
application for a data center use that has been officially accepted for review in accordance with
applicable Loudoun County ordinances and policies on or before the Effective Date, may continue
to process their administrative application without obtaining a special exception for the data center
use, provided that (i) the data center use was permitted by-right at the time the administrative
application was officially accepted for review, (ii) the proposed structures and external mechanical
equipment for the data center use would have been more than five hundred feet from all residential
structures existing on the Effective Date, and (iii) the applicant does not make any substantial
modification(s) to such administrative application (as defined in Paragraph 8 below). Subject to
the review and approval of the Director of the Office of Mapping and Geographic Information,
applicants must submit evidence to demonstrate that the proposed structures and external
mechanical equipment for a data center use would have been more than five hundred feet from all
residential structures existing on the Effective Date. Upon approval, a grandfathered administrative
application will be treated for purposes of the Zoning Ordinance as if the administrative application
had been approved prior to the Effective Date.

       3.    The following legislative applications that have been officially accepted for review
as of the Effective Date may be eligible for grandfathering under this resolution: zoning map
amendments, zoning concept plan amendments, and zoning conversions in the Route 28 tax
district.

        4.     A legislative application will be considered officially accepted for review when
(i) all minimum submission requirements identified on the applicable checklist have either been
satisfied as determined by Department of Planning and Zoning (“DPZ”) staff or waived by the
Director of DPZ; and (ii) the applicable application fee has been received by the County.
                                                                                         Page 2 of 5

       5.     The following administrative applications that have been officially accepted for
review as of the Effective Date may be eligible for grandfathering under this resolution: site plans,
and site plan amendments.

        6.    An administrative application will be considered officially accepted for review when
(i) all required or minimum submission items identified on the applicable checklist have been
satisfied as determined by Department of Building and Development staff; and (ii) the applicable
application fee has been received by the County.

       7.    For purposes of this resolution, a “substantial modification” to a legislative application
is a modification made after the Effective Date that:

              (a)     would require a new special exception application in order to increase floor
                      area ratio;

              (b)     would require a new special exception application in order to increase the
                      height for a building housing a data center use;

              (c)     would require a new modification pursuant to Section 10.10.04.C of the
                      Zoning Ordinance or a new minor special exception application in order to
                      decrease the setback for a building housing a data center use;

              (d)     changes or adds proposed uses;

              (e)     changes or increases the land area subject to the legislative application; or

              (f)     otherwise requires a legislative application to be remanded to the Planning
                      Commission for further consideration, as determined by the Office of the
                      County Attorney.

      However, even if a modification is of the kind described in (a) – (f) above, such modification
shall not be a substantial modification to a legislative application if it was requested by or made in
response to a recommendation adopted by the Board or Planning Commission in an open meeting, or
a comment from County staff in an official referral or review memorandum.

       8.     For purposes of this resolution, a “substantial modification” to an administrative
application is a modification made after the Effective Date that:

              (a)     increases proposed square footage of a data center use by more than five
                      percent (5%);

              (b)     results in a floor area ratio that exceeds the maximum allowed in the district;

              (c)     increases the height for a building housing a data center use;

              (d)     decreases the setback for a building housing a data center use to less than the
                      setback required in the zoning district or less than the 500-foot standard in
                      clause (ii) of Paragraph 2;

              (e)     changes or adds proposed uses; or

              (f)     changes or increases the land area subject to the administrative application.

      However, even if a modification is of the kind described in (a) – (f) above, such modification
shall not be a substantial modification to an administrative application if it was requested by or
made in response to a comment from County staff in an official referral or review memorandum.

       9.    An application may retain its grandfathered status for so long as the applicant
diligently pursues approval of such application, as prescribed below. An application is not

                      For legislative applications, (i) the applicant fails to resubmit application
                      materials for further review or consideration within six months after a
                      request for resubmission by the assigned project manager for the application
                      (“Project Manager”); (ii) the applicant fails to submit any reports or other
                      materials in support of an application within six months after request for such
                      materials by the Project Manager; (iii) the applicant fails timely to provide
                      the requisite notices for a scheduled public hearing; (iv) the applicant fails to
                      submit application materials (e.g., updated plans or proffers, etc.) for a
                      scheduled public hearing, work session, business meeting or committee
                      meeting (as applicable) within the prescribed meeting deadlines as
                      determined by the Office of the County Administrator; or (v) an application
                      is either withdrawn or becomes inactive in accordance with Section 10.01.H
                      and I of the Zoning Ordinance.

                      For administrative applications, (i) the applicant fails to resubmit application
                      materials for further review or consideration within six months after request
                      for resubmission by the Project Manager; (ii) the applicant fails to submit
                      any reports or other materials in support of an application within six months
                      after request for such materials by the Project Manager; (iii) an application
                      is either withdrawn by the applicant or becomes inactive in accordance with
                      Section 1242.15 of the Land Subdivision and Development Ordinance of
                      Loudoun County, Virginia (LSDO); or (iv) the applicant fails to submit
                      application materials that comply with all applicable Zoning Ordinance,

    RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF SUPERVISORS OF LOUDOUN COUNTY,
  VIRGINIA, ADOPTING GRANDFATHERING RULES FOR ZOAM-2024-0001, DATA
               CENTER STANDARDS AND LOCATIONS, PHASE I

         WHEREAS, on July 2, 2024, the Board of Supervisors of Loudoun County, Virginia
 (“Board”), adopted a resolution initiating amendments to the existing Loudoun County Zoning
 Ordinance (“Zoning Ordinance”) for the purpose of requiring approval of a special exception prior
 to the establishment of a data center use in the Industrial Park (IP), General Industry (GI), and
 Mineral Resource – Heavy Industry (MR-HI) zoning districts; and

       WHEREAS, the project to amend the zoning ordinance is known as ZOAM-2024-0001,
 Data Center Standards and Locations, Phase 1; and

         WHEREAS, after a period of extensive public engagement, including input from affected
 regulatory agencies and departments, stakeholder groups, residents, business owners, and other
 interested parties, Ccounty staff prepared draft zoning ordinance text amendments (“Text
 Amendments”) for consideration by the Planning Commission; and

        WHEREAS, the Planning Commission conducted one public hearing on September 24,
 2024, and 6 work sessions on the Text Amendments over a period of approximately four months;
 and

         WHEREAS, on December 12, 2024, the Planning Commission forwarded (5-3-1: Barnes,
 Frank, and Keirce opposed; Jasper absent) the Text Amendments to the Board with a
 recommendation of denial, and with the further recommendation that the Board adopt a
 grandfathering resolution effective as of the Board public hearing date if the Text Amendments are
 to be adopted; and

       WHEREAS, on February 12, 2025, the Board conducted a public hearing on the Text
 Amendments and further discussed the Text Amendments and grandfathering resolution at its
 March 4, 2025, and March 18, 2025, Business Meetings; and

       WHEREAS, on March 18, 2025, the Board adopted the final Text Amendments, as amended
by any changes or corrections approved by the Board at the time of adoption; and



        WHEREAS, the Board has determined that the orderly transition from the prior provisions of
Table 3.02.05-1 of the Zoning Ordinance (“Prior Table”) to the new provisions for Table 3.02.05-1
of the Zoning Ordinance (“New Table”) would be assisted by the implementation of rules allowing
development applications for a data center use that were officially accepted for review, in accordance
with applicable County ordinances and policies, on or before February 12, 2025, to be processed
without requiring a special exception for the data center use under the Prior Table (“Grandfathering
Rules”); and

         WHEREAS, Section 1.02.G. of the Zoning Ordinance provides, in part, that the “Board of
 Supervisors may establish grandfathering provisions by resolution adopted contemporaneously
 with this Zoning Ordinance or any amendment”; and.

       WHEREAS, the County officially accepting a legislative or administrative application for
review is the earliest point in the review and approval process that balances the reasonable
expectations of property owners seeking development approvals with the need to adopt zoning
ordinance amendments to protect other property owners and the community from the adverse
impacts of conflicting land uses; and

        WHEREAS, requiring a minimum 500-foot buffer from a proposed data center structure to
a residential structure existing on the Effective Date (defined below) is good land use and zoning
practice that provides residential areas with healthy surroundings for family life, promotes an
attractive, convenient and harmonious community, protects the health, safety and general welfare
of the public, and provides a minimum buffer area to mitigate the impacts of conflicting land uses
caused by the size, height, area, bulk, and location of data center structures, the noise generated
from such structures, and the obstruction of light, air, and view by such structures.

       NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the Board of Supervisors of Loudoun County,
Virginia that:

      1.     Subject to the terms and conditions of this resolution, applicants of a legislative and
administrative applications for a data center use that hasve been officially accepted for review in
accordance with applicable Loudoun County ordinances and policies on or before February 12,
2025 (“Effective Date”), may continue to be processed under the Prior Table their legislative
application without applying for a special exception for the data center use (i.e., grandfathered),
provided the applicant does not make any substantial modification(s) to such legislative
application (as defined in Paragraph 6 7 below). Upon approval, a grandfathered legislative
application will be treated for purposes of the Zoning Ordinance as if the legislative application
had been approved prior to the Effective Date.

       2.      Subject to the terms and conditions of this resolution, applicants of an administrative
application for a data center use that has been officially accepted for review in accordance with
applicable Loudoun County ordinances and policies on or before the Effective Date, may continue
to process their administrative application without obtaining a special exception for the data center
use, provided that (i) the data center use was permitted by-right at the time the administrative
application was officially accepted for review, (ii) the proposed structures and external mechanical
equipment for the data center use would have been more than five hundred feet from all residential
structures existing on the Effective Date, and (iii) the applicant does not make any substantial
modification(s) to such administrative application (as defined in Paragraph 8 below). Subject to
the review and approval of the Director of the Office of Mapping and Geographic Information,
applicants must submit evidence to demonstrate that the proposed structures and external
mechanical equipment for a data center use would have been more than five hundred feet from all
residential structures existing on the Effective Date. Upon approval, a grandfathered administrative
application will be treated for purposes of the Zoning Ordinance as if the administrative application
had been approved prior to the Effective Date.
