          Briskman Advances Proposal to Pause
         Data Center and Substation Applications
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Contact: Aman Ardalan
Senior Legislative Aide to Supervisor Briskman
aman.ardalan@loudoun.gov

LEESBURG, VA, July 23, 2026 – Algonkian District Supervisor Juli E. Briskman introduced a
successful motion at last night’s Board of Supervisors Business Meeting that could allow
Loudoun County to pause data center applications, site plans, and substation applications
while the County completes zoning amendments relating to the industry.

The motion directed County staff to return to the Board at its September 15 Business
Meeting with an item establishing a pause and providing legal analysis from the County
Attorney’s Office.
“Data centers have become an increasingly important part of our local economy, but their
unchecked sprawl has raised serious concerns about impacts on surrounding communities
and the growing demands on our energy grid and natural resources,” said Supervisor
Briskman. “Loudoun became the data center capital of the world in large part because data
center development occurred with little oversight and before the full impacts were studied
or understood. This pause would offer the County an opportunity to conduct its due
diligence and establish appropriate policies, regulations, and standards so that we can
better protect our communities and natural resources.”
Loudoun County would not be the first Virginia jurisdiction to pause data center applications
while reviewing land-use policies. In September 2025, the Fluvanna County Board of
Supervisors approved a moratorium on new data center applications while it considered
changes to its zoning regulations. Suffolk City Council is also considering a moratorium, and
Front Royal Town Council ordered a pause last month. Chesapeake City Council approved
an eight-month pause just this week.
The Data Center Standards and Locations initiative began in 2022, after Supervisor Briskman
proposed a comprehensive remapping project in 2021. Phase 1, completed in March 2025,
updated the County’s Zoning Ordinance to require proposed data centers to obtain special
exception approval, including a vote by the Board of Supervisors.

Phase 2 began in September 2025 and focuses on establishing new policy guidance and
use-specific standards for data centers and utility substations. The review also includes
data center compatibility and siting, utility substation policies, and the possibility of onsite
power generation and battery storage. The Board will also consider noise, building heights,
parking, and other standards governing future development. Completion of Phase 2 is
anticipated in Spring 2027.
With more than 250 data centers, Loudoun County hosts approximately 53 million square
feet of data center development, more than double the amount recorded in 2021.
Additionally, the Board of Supervisors approved a grandfathering resolution in 2025 that
allowed more than 20 data center administrative applications to remain in the by-right
approval pipeline. Briskman opposed this grandfathering, and her motion to require a
legislative process for those administrative applications failed.
Data centers generate more than $1 billion in annual local tax revenue and support
thousands of jobs, but their continued growth has crowded out alternative economic
development and housing opportunities and created significant demand for new energy grid
infrastructure.
"I have heard from residents across the county who want data center growth severely
constrained or stopped entirely. Loudouners are tired of the visual and noise impacts,
concerned about the continued expansion of energy infrastructure and rising utility costs,
and worried about the air they breathe,” said Supervisor Briskman. “It is time we take back
control of land use decisions in this county, pause all new data center applications and site
plans, and complete this work to establish a more balanced and responsible framework for
considering data centers and related energy infrastructure applications.”
More information about the Data Center Standards and Locations initiative is available at
https://www.loudoun.gov/5990/Data-Center-Standards-Locations.

Supervisor Juli E. Briskman was first elected in 2019 to represent the Algonkian District on
the Board of Supervisors and was reelected in 2023. She served as Vice Chair of the Board
in 2024 and as Chair of the Finance, Government Operations, and Economic Development
Committee for nearly two years. Briskman is a proud mom of two Loudoun County Public
Schools alumni and is a graduate of The Ohio State University. She also holds an MBA from
Johns Hopkins University. Briskman represented Loudoun on the Metropolitan Washington
Council of Governments for four years and currently serves on the Northern Virginia
Transportation Commission and the Virginia Association of Counties Board of Directors.
